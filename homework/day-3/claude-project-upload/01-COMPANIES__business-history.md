# Kasim Aslam: Business History

*A written history compiled from Kasim's own public posts (LinkedIn and Facebook, exported 2026-09-24), the Pareto Talent Bootcamp Day 3 class (2026-09-23), and the delegation research doc (2026-09-23). Nothing here is invented. Gaps are marked.*

## 1. The whiteboard at 19
Kasim's story, as he tells it, starts with a prediction of failure. At 19 he sat in the corner office of "the smartest man I knew": a man with the secretary, the Ivy League degree and the authority. That man drew Kasim's future on a whiteboard in colored markers, branch by branch, "until he'd proven it like a mathematician," and Kasim walked out "certain I was doomed." For years he looked for the error in the proof. He never found it: "The logic was airtight." He tells this story under #foundermindset and #mentorship, and it frames everything after. He went on to build anyway.

## 4. Ivan and the team he never met
One of Kasim's hires was [[ivan-bunin|Ivan Bunin]], "a Ukrainian college student studying in Poland." Kasim calls Ivan the best example of what he's proudest of in his career. In the bootcamp, Ivan says he started as Kasim's executive assistant, checking his emails. Kasim later posted that his **former assistant is now the CEO of a multi-million-dollar business** that has passed **$1,000,000 in salaries paid to people in emerging nations**. A companion YouTube title reads "I Built a $3,000,000 Team in Argentina Without Meeting Them." That business is very likely [[pareto-talent|Pareto Talent]]: co-founded by Kasim, run by Ivan as CEO, about $3M ARR, placing Latin American executive assistants with US founders. On Jun 17, 2026 the team reached **100 executive assistants placed**, and the goal is 300 within 12 months.

## 5. What he learned about delegation
Kasim's delegation philosophy (see [[delegation-philosophy]]) came from the same journey. If your team only does as well as you would have, "you delegated wrong." Step-by-step instructions freeze your old knowledge into their ceiling, so give people the destination instead. He warns about the "Control Paradox" ("you don't have a time problem, you have a control problem"), about "identity fusion" (not knowing where you end and your business begins), and says the founder is usually the bottleneck. He has also started talking about what *not* to delegate: relationships, vision and consequences.

## 6. The AI turn (2026)
In 2026 Kasim's public voice shifted almost entirely to AI. He started [[the-daily-sigh|The Daily Sigh]], a free daily live mastermind featuring guests like Tommy Mello, John Moran, Kamal Ravikant and Jake Van Clief. He ran a sold-out one-day "AI That Actually Works" session in Austin (Apr 3, 20 seats), and he announced a two-day AI leadership workshop in Phoenix (Oct 15–16, $2k). He writes openly about feeling "terror and excitement, every single day" since AI arrived, and calls himself a "neurotic, armchair doomsday prepper." That feeling led him to his current series: AI as an **economic mass-extinction event** that will wipe out most businesses (75%+), and what the surviving 25% have in common (see [[ai-extinction-series]]). His message to founders: don't try to become the best AI user. Become the best leader of people who use AI (see [[ai-leadership-framework]]).
